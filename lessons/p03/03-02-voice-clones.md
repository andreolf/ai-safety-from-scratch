The US Federal Trade Commission warns that a scammer needs only a short audio clip, perhaps from a video posted online, to call you in a voice that sounds like a family member. The script is old: an accident, the police, money needed now, don’t tell anyone. The voice is new.

A [[term:voice-clone]] can make a scammer sound like someone you love. So the voice is no longer proof of who is calling. Check the story through a channel you trust, not the call itself.

How a voice clone call is put together, step by step.
:::end

## How it works

The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) explains it in one sentence:

:::source url="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/scammers-use-fake-emergencies-steal-your-money" by="US Federal Trade Commission, Scammers Use Fake Emergencies To Steal Your Money" date="consumer advice page" checked="26 Oct 2026"
With a short audio clip — maybe from content posted online — and a voice-cloning program, a scammer could call you and sound just like your family member.
:::end

The FBI’s December 2024 warning describes the same thing: criminals “generate short audio clips containing a loved one’s voice to impersonate a close relative in a crisis situation, asking for immediate financial assistance or demanding a ransom.”

## The pattern to recognise

The FTC lists what fake emergency calls have in common:

- **Urgency:** it is urgent, and “you’re the only one who can help”.
- **Secrecy:** they tell you to keep it secret, so you don’t talk to family who would spot the scam.
- **Emotion:** they count on you acting quickly, “without stopping to check out whether there’s really an emergency.”
- **Hard-to-reverse payment:** wire transfers, cryptocurrency, payment apps or gift card numbers.
- Sometimes an **“authority figure”** joins in: a fake lawyer, police officer or doctor.

The AI part is new. The pattern is old. That is good news: you do not need to detect the fake voice. You only need to recognise the pattern and check.

## What to do

The FTC’s advice is direct: “don’t trust the voice on the line — even if it sounds like your family member or friend.” Then:

1. **Resist the pressure.** Hang up, or say you will call right back.
2. **Call back on a number you know** is right for that person.
3. **Call someone else in the family**, even if you were told to keep it secret.
4. If you don’t want to hang up, **ask a question only the real person would know.** Better still, agree a family code word in advance (Lesson 03.06).

:::intern fooled
The intern’s “sister” calls from an unknown number: phone stolen, stuck abroad, needs money for a ticket today. The voice is perfect. The intern almost pays, then texts the sister’s normal number. She is at home, cooking dinner.
---
A perfect voice proves nothing. A call back on a number you already know proves a lot.
:::end

:::exercise Write your script
Write the exact words you will say if you get an emergency call asking for money, so you don’t have to think under pressure. Keep it to two sentences.
:::solution
Example: “I want to help, so I’m going to hang up and call you straight back on your usual number.” And: “If it’s really you, tell me our family word.” Then hang up and call the person, or another family member, on a number you already have. A ready-made script beats deciding in a panic.
:::end
