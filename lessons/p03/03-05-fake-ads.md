## The hook

You are scrolling and see a news-style video: a well-known TV presenter explains how they made a fortune with a new trading platform, and you can too, starting with just a few hundred francs. It looks like a real interview. It isn’t.

## The one idea

**Generative AI makes it cheap to put a famous person’s face and voice on an ad they never made. Treat any celebrity endorsement of an investment or miracle product as fake until proven otherwise, and never pay through a link in an ad.**

## What the FBI describes

The FBI’s December 2024 warning lists several examples that fit together into this scam:

- Images “of celebrities or social media personas promoting counterfeit products or non-delivery schemes”.
- “Believable depictions of public figures to bolster their fraud schemes.”
- Videos “for fictitious or misleading promotional materials for investment fraud schemes.”
- AI-written content “for fraudulent websites for cryptocurrency investment fraud and other investment schemes.”

:::source url="https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203" by="FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center, Alert I-120324-PSA" date="3 Dec 2024" checked="26 Oct 2026"

## How the scam usually unfolds

1. **The hook:** an ad or fake news article with a famous face.
2. **The form:** you leave your phone number “to learn more”.
3. **The friendly advisor:** someone calls, helpful and patient, and helps you open an account.
4. **The fake dashboard:** a website or app shows your “profits” growing, so you invest more.
5. **The trap:** when you try to withdraw, there are fees, taxes or “unlock” payments. The money is gone.

Steps 3 to 5 are covered in detail in Security from Scratch (lessons 08.03 and 08.04). AI makes step 1 more convincing and cheaper to produce in every language.

## Questions that expose it

- **Would this person really sell this?** Famous people are not giving away investment tips in ads.
- **Is it on their official channels?** Check their verified accounts or website, not the ad.
- **“Guaranteed”, “risk-free”, “limited time”?** Real investments carry risk, and nobody guarantees returns.
- **Is the firm authorised?** Check the financial regulator’s list in your country before sending money.

:::exercise Run the four questions
An ad shows a well-known business leader saying: “My new platform guarantees 30% a month. Places are limited, sign up today.” Apply the four questions and decide what to do.
:::solution
1. A business leader personally selling a platform in a social ad is very unlikely. 2. It will not be on their official channels; check anyway. 3. “Guarantees 30% a month” and “places are limited” are classic red flags: no real investment guarantees returns like that. 4. The firm will not be on the regulator’s list of authorised firms. Action: don’t click, report the ad to the platform, and warn anyone who shared it.
:::end
