## The hook

Every lesson in this phase ended the same way. Not “look closer”, but “check another way”. That is not a coincidence. It is the most important idea in this phase.

## The one idea

**Don’t rely on spotting fakes. Verify: hang up, call back on a number you know, use a family [[term:code-word|code word]], and ask someone you trust. Verification works however good the fake is.**

:::figure verify-not-spot
Two strategies side by side. One gets weaker as AI improves; the other doesn’t.
:::end

## Why spotting is a losing game

The FBI does list visual clues, such as “distorted hands or feet, unrealistic teeth or eyes” and “lag time, voice matching, and unrealistic movements”. They are worth knowing, and they sometimes help. But the same alert says “it can be difficult to identify when content is AI-generated”, and the Arup worker in Lesson 03.03 saw colleagues who “looked and sounded just like” the real people. Clues get fixed with each new version of the tools. A habit of checking does not go out of date.

## The four verification moves

All four come from official advice, the FBI (December 2024) and the FTC:

1. **Hang up and call back** on a number you find yourself. The FBI: verify “by hanging up the phone, researching the contact of the bank or organization purporting to call you, and call the phone number directly.”
2. **Use a family code word.** The FBI: “Create a secret word or phrase with your family to verify their identity.”
3. **Tell someone else.** The FTC: call someone else in your family or circle of friends, “even if the caller said to keep it a secret”.
4. **Never pay in a hurry**, especially by wire, crypto, payment app or gift card, the methods the FTC says scammers insist on because they are “tough to get your money back”.

The family code word, from the course’s infographic set.
:::end

## Make the code word work

- **Choose it together**, in person: something easy to remember, hard to guess, and never posted online (not a pet’s name that appears on social media).
- **Agree the rule:** anyone asking for money or help in an emergency must say the word. No word, no money, whoever they sound like.
- **Practise once**, so it feels normal rather than rude to ask.
- The FBI also suggests you limit online content of your image and voice, and make social accounts private, so there is less material to copy.

## Try the lab

The Call-Back Drill plays a panicked “family emergency” call in a cloned voice. You score points for the right moves, not for guessing whether it is fake.

:::lab callback-drill
:::intern proud
The intern’s “nephew” calls in tears: he crashed a hire car abroad and needs CHF 3,000 for the damage, today. The intern says, kindly: “What’s our family word?” Silence. The caller hangs up. The intern calls the nephew’s usual number: he is fine, at university.
---
The code word worked without the intern needing to spot anything. That is the whole point.
:::end

:::exercise Set it up this week
Plan your family code word: who needs to know it, where you will agree it (in person or on a call you started), and the one-sentence rule you will all follow.
:::solution
Example: “Me, my parents and my sister. We agree it at Sunday lunch, in person. Rule: anyone asking for money or urgent help must say the word; if they can’t, we hang up and call back on the number we already have.” Write the rule down for older relatives, and remind them that a real family member will never be offended by being asked.
:::end
