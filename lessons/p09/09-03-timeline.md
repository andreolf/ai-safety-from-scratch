An article from 2025 tells you the EU’s high-risk AI rules start on 2 August 2026. A website from 2026 says 2 December 2027. Both were right when they were written. Laws change, and AI law has already changed once.

The EU AI Act applies step by step: it entered into force on 1 August 2024, with milestones until 2 August 2028. In 2026 it was amended by the Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, which moved the high-risk dates later and added new bans. Always check which version of the timeline you are reading.

:::figure act-timeline
The timeline as published by the AI Act Service Desk, including the Omnibus changes.
:::end

## The timeline, as amended

From the AI Act Service Desk, which says its timeline “takes into account the AI Act amendments introduced by Digital Omnibus on AI”:

- **1 August 2024:** entry into force.
- **2 February 2025:** definitions, AI literacy and the first prohibitions apply.
- **2 August 2025:** rules for general-purpose AI models; governance must be in place.
- **2 August 2026:** “the majority of rules of the AI Act come into force and enforcement starts”, including the transparency rules of Article 50.
- **2 December 2026:** new prohibitions on AI generating non-consensual sexual deepfakes and child sexual abuse material; a transition deadline for some generative AI already on the market.
- **2 August 2027:** each Member State should have at least one AI regulatory sandbox running.
- **2 December 2027:** rules for high-risk AI in the Annex III areas.
- **2 August 2028:** rules for high-risk AI built into products covered by Annex I, such as lifts or toys.

As of this lesson’s check on 26 October 2026, the next milestone is 2 December 2026.

## What the Digital Omnibus changed

Before the amendment, the Commission’s own FAQ notes, the remaining parts of the Act “are set to apply on 2 August 2026 and 2 August 2027.” Stakeholder consultations throughout 2025 “revealed implementation challenges”, and the Commission wanted the high-risk rules to apply “when companies have the right support tools to facilitate implementation, such as standards.”

:::source url="https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai" by="European Commission, AI Act" date="" checked="26 Oct 2026"
This legislative proposal (dubbed as the ' AI Omnibus') was adopted on 19 November 2025, a political agreement was reached on 7 May 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026.
:::end

The final result, written into Article 113:

- Annex III high-risk rules: from **2 December 2027** (originally 2 August 2026).
- Annex I high-risk rules: from **2 August 2028** (originally 2 August 2027).
- New bans in Article 5 (points (ba) and (bb)): from **2 December 2026**.

Other changes listed by the Commission include extending some simplifications for small and medium-sized enterprises to small mid-caps, more access to regulatory sandboxes including an EU-level one, and stronger AI Office oversight of AI systems built on general-purpose models.

**Proposal versus law.** The Commission’s 2025 proposal would have linked the high-risk start dates to when standards became available, with a cap of “maximum 16 months later than originally envisaged” for Annex III. You may still find that wording in older articles and FAQs. The adopted text sets fixed dates instead, which the Commission describes as “a clear implementation timeline”.

## Which text is the law?

The AI Act Explorer states that its legal text “is based on the EUR-Lex consolidated version as at 27 July 2026”, and adds: “For the legally authentic text, consult the original AI Act and the Digital Omnibus on AI as published in the Official Journal.” A consolidated version is a convenient merged copy. The official versions are the two regulations as published.

:::exercise Date check
Your company sells (a) an AI tool that screens job applicants and (b) a chatbot. Using the amended timeline, from when does each main set of rules apply?
:::solution
(a) Screening job applicants is Annex III high-risk: the high-risk rules apply from 2 December 2027. (b) The chatbot’s transparency duty under Article 50(1), telling people they are talking to an AI, has applied since 2 August 2026. If the chatbot generates synthetic content and was already on the market before 2 August 2026, its machine-readable marking duty under Article 50(2) has a transition until 2 December 2026.
:::end
