## The hook

The EU AI Act is law across the European Union. But AI doesn’t stop at borders, and many countries, including Switzerland, the UK and the US, are not in the EU. Is there anything they all agreed to?

## The one idea

**The Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law (CETS No. 225) is a treaty: countries sign it and then commit to make their own laws meet its principles. It does not have the EU’s risk tiers. It sets shared principles and rights, and leaves the details to each country.**

## What it is

The Council of Europe is a different organisation from the European Union. It has 46 member states, and it works on human rights, democracy and the rule of law.

:::source url="https://www.coe.int/en/web/artificial-intelligence/the-framework-convention-on-artificial-intelligence" by="Council of Europe, The Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence" date="" checked="26 Oct 2026"
The Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and human rights, democracy and the rule of law is the first-ever international legally binding treaty in this field. Opened for signature on 5 September 2024, it aims to ensure that activities within the lifecycle of artificial intelligence systems are fully consistent with human rights, democracy and the rule of law, while being conducive to technological progress and innovation.
:::end

It was opened for signature in Vilnius. It was drafted by the Council’s 46 member states with observer states including Canada, Japan, Mexico, the Holy See and the United States, plus the EU and other countries. When this lesson was checked, the signatories listed included Switzerland, the United Kingdom, Canada, Israel, Japan, the United States, Uruguay and the European Union.

## What it asks for

The Council of Europe summarises the obligations in three groups:

- **Principles:** human dignity and individual autonomy; equality and non-discrimination; privacy and personal data protection; transparency and oversight; accountability and responsibility; reliability; safe innovation.
- **Remedies and rights:** documenting how AI systems are used so people can challenge decisions; the right to complain to an authority; and “provision of notice that one is interacting with an artificial intelligence system and not with a human being.”
- **Risk management:** risk and impact assessments, and the possibility for authorities to ban or pause certain uses (“red lines”).

It covers AI used by public authorities and by private actors, but countries can choose how to apply it to the private sector: directly, or through “other measures”. National security and defence are largely outside its scope.

## How it becomes binding

Signing is a first step. The treaty enters into force three months after five signatories, including at least three Council of Europe member states, have formally agreed to be bound by it (ratification). Each country then answers to the others through a “Conference of the Parties” that checks implementation.

The intern reads that Switzerland signed the Convention and concludes: “Great, so the EU AI Act now applies in Switzerland.”

Different organisation, different instrument. The Convention is a treaty on principles; each country writes its own laws to meet it. Lesson 09.05 shows what Switzerland is doing.
:::end
