A Swiss start-up builds an AI hiring tool. Which rules apply: Swiss law, the EU AI Act, both, or none? The answer depends on where its customers are, and Switzerland’s own rules are still being written.

Switzerland is not copying the EU AI Act. It will bring the Council of Europe AI Convention into Swiss law, changing existing laws sector by sector where possible, with general rules only for key areas such as data protection. Meanwhile, existing Swiss law applies, and Swiss companies selling AI into the EU must follow the EU AI Act.

## The decision of February 2025

On 22 November 2023, the Federal Council asked two departments for an overview of possible ways to regulate AI. On 12 February 2025, it decided its approach. The Federal Office of Communications (OFCOM) describes three objectives: “strengthening Switzerland as a location for innovation, safeguarding the protection of fundamental rights, including economic freedom, and increasing public trust in AI.”

:::source url="https://www.uvek.admin.ch/en/nsb?id=104110" by="Swiss Federal Council (DETEC), AI regulation: Federal Council to ratify Council of Europe Convention" date="12 Feb 2025" checked="26 Oct 2026"
Where legislative changes are needed, they should be as sector-specific as possible. Only key areas relevant to fundamental rights, such as data protection, will be subject to general, cross-sectoral regulation.
:::end

The same release says the Convention “will apply primarily to state actors”, and that work on AI rules also continues in sectors such as healthcare and transport. Besides laws, the Federal Council wants non-binding measures, such as “self-disclosure agreements or industry solutions”.

## Signing the Convention

On 27 March 2025, Federal Councillor Albert Rösti signed the Council of Europe Convention for Switzerland in Strasbourg. The government noted it had been “adopted in 2024 with Switzerland playing a key role”.

## Where things stand (checked 26 October 2026)

The federal SME Portal summarised the position on 23 September 2026:

- “To date, Switzerland has no overarching legislation specifically governing AI.” But AI is “not developing in a legal vacuum”: companies must follow existing law such as the Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) and sector rules.
- Three federal departments “will draw up a consultation draft in spring 2027”, covering “transparency, data protection, non-discrimination and supervision.” (Earlier government pages said the end of 2026. The timetable has moved.)
- “Swiss companies that place AI systems or general-purpose AI models on the European Union market are subject to the EU AI Act. Providers and deployers from third countries may also be subject to the Act if the output generated by an AI system is used in the EU.”

So the Swiss start-up from the hook must follow Swiss data protection law at home and, if it sells its hiring tool in the EU, the AI Act’s high-risk rules from 2 December 2027.

EU rules follow the market. If your AI or its output is used in the EU, check the AI Act, wherever you are based.

:::exercise Which rules?
A company in Zurich offers (a) an AI chatbot used only by Swiss customers and (b) the same chatbot sold to a bank in Milan. Which rules should it check in each case?
:::solution
(a) Swiss law: the Federal Act on Data Protection and any sector rules (for example banking rules if relevant). No Swiss AI-specific law exists yet; a consultation draft is planned for spring 2027. (b) Swiss law at home, plus the EU AI Act, because the system is placed on the EU market: at least the Article 50 transparency duty for chatbots, and possibly more depending on what the bank uses it for.
:::end
