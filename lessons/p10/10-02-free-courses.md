## The hook

You finish this course and want more. A search for “AI safety course” returns paid bootcamps, long reading lists and confident influencers. Which free options are worth your time, and which one first?

## The one idea

**Pick the next step that matches your time and background. A short free introduction first; videos if you want to see inside the models; a longer free cohort course if you want to work on the problem. Check what a course asks of you before you sign up.**

:::figure learning-ladder
A ladder of free next steps, from two hours to six weeks.
:::end

## Step 1: a two-hour introduction

BlueDot Impact, an organisation that runs AI safety courses, offers a self-paced course called The Future of AI:

:::source url="https://bluedot.org/courses/future-of-ai" by="BlueDot Impact, The Future of AI" date="" checked="26 Oct 2026"
An introduction to what AI can do today, where it's going over the next decade, and how you can start contributing to a better future.
:::end

Its page says “Get informed in 2 hours”, “No prerequisites. No technical knowledge required.” and “Completely free. No hidden costs.”

## Step 2: see inside the models

- **3Blue1Brown’s neural network series:** animated videos that show what a neural network is and how it learns. Its lesson on transformers explains the technology behind chatbots.
- **Andrej Karpathy’s one-hour talk “Intro to Large Language Models”:** a researcher explains training, assistants and security risks such as jailbreaks and prompt injection. We quoted it in lessons 04.03 and 08.01.

## Step 3: a cohort course

BlueDot also runs free courses with a group and a facilitator. Two examples:

- **Technical AI Safety:** for people with a background in machine learning, computer science or maths, surveying alignment, interpretability, evaluations and control. Intensive (about 6 days at 5 hours a day) or part-time (about 6 weeks at 5 hours a week).
- **Frontier AI Governance:** “Online · 40 hours · Intensive or part-time · Free”, for technical people exploring governance and for professionals in policy, law, security, economics or journalism.

:::source url="https://bluedot.org/courses/ai-governance" by="BlueDot Impact, Frontier AI Governance" date="" checked="26 Oct 2026"
Build the judgment to help shape how frontier AI is governed. Assess evidence, compare competing strategies, and test your ideas through practical exercises and discussion.
:::end

Cohort courses have application rounds with deadlines that change, so check the course page for the current dates.

## Before you sign up anywhere

Ask the same questions you would ask about any claim (lesson 00.04): who runs it, what does it cost, what will you be asked to share, and what does the certificate really mean? This course’s own certificate is a free certificate of completion, not a qualification.

:::intern fooled
The intern found a “Certified AI Safety Expert” bootcamp: 1,990 francs, three days, a badge for LinkedIn. A free two-hour course covered most of the same ground.
---
Free and well-known first. Pay only when you know exactly what you are getting, and who stands behind the certificate.
:::end

:::exercise Pick your next step
You have three hours a week for the next month and no technical background. Choose a plan from this lesson and write it as three steps with dates.
:::solution
Example: Week 1: BlueDot’s two-hour Future of AI course. Weeks 2–3: the first videos of 3Blue1Brown’s neural network series, one or two a week. Week 4: Karpathy’s one-hour talk. Then decide whether to apply for a cohort course such as Frontier AI Governance. A technical cohort course would not fit yet, because it expects a background in machine learning, computer science or maths.
:::end
