This course covers AI security: how AI systems get attacked and go wrong, and how to protect them. The three places below go further in directions it does not. I recommend all three. The [Where to go next](#/next) page keeps the same list, linked from the footer, so you can find it later.

## BlueDot Impact

BlueDot Impact is a non-profit that runs free AI safety courses. Its home page says more than 10,000 people have taken one. Three fit well after this course:

- **The Future of AI.** Self-paced, about two hours, no application and no technical background. Its page: “No prerequisites. No technical knowledge required.” A good first step.
- **AGI Strategy.** About 25 hours in a small group led by a facilitator, either intensive over about five days or part-time over about five weeks. It is BlueDot’s main course on strategy for advanced AI, and the one it suggests before the technical course.
- **Technical AI Safety.** About six days intensive or six weeks part-time. It goes deep on topics from Phases 07 and 08 here:

:::source url="https://bluedot.org/courses/technical-ai-safety" by="BlueDot Impact, Technical AI Safety" date="" checked="27 Oct 2026"
alignment and RLHF, mechanistic interpretability, evaluations and red-teaming, AI control, and scalable oversight
:::end

BlueDot recommends AGI Strategy before Technical AI Safety. Cohort courses have application rounds, so check each page for current dates.

## ARENA

ARENA trains people to do alignment research. It runs in-person bootcamps of four to five weeks in London, two or three times a year, and its whole curriculum is free online as Python notebooks: fundamentals, interpretability, reinforcement learning, evals and alignment science. You need to be comfortable with Python and some maths. If lesson 08.02’s feature dial made you want to find features yourself, this is where you learn how.

## Jailbroken

Jailbroken is a game for AI red-teaming: “a playable academy for AI security” with live, model-backed targets and a shared Daily Challenge. This course’s labs are simulations that explain why an attack works. Jailbroken is where you practise on real models (lesson 04.06).

## Also worth your time

- **3Blue1Brown’s neural network series:** animated videos on how neural networks learn, including one on transformers.
- **Andrej Karpathy’s one-hour talk “Intro to Large Language Models”:** training, assistants, and a closing section on jailbreaks and prompt injection. Lessons 04.03 and 08.02 quote it.
